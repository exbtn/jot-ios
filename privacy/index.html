Privacy Policy for Jot

Effective date: October 26, 2026

Jot ("the app," "we," "us") is a personal note-taking and memory app for iOS. This policy explains what information Jot collects, how it is used, and who it is shared with. It applies to the Jot iOS app and its backend services.

The data controller is Yevhenii Veretennikov, a sole trader (jednoosobowa działalność gospodarcza) registered in CEIDG in Poland — full registration details are in the Terms of Use.

If you have questions about this policy or want to exercise any of the rights described below, contact us at veret.evg@gmail.com.

Information We Collect

Account information

Jot can be used anonymously, or signed in with Apple or with Google.

  • Before you sign in, Jot creates a random identifier stored securely on your device (in the iOS Keychain) so your notes can be saved and later linked to an account if you sign in.
  • If you sign in with Apple, we receive an identity token from Apple and the anonymous user identifier inside it, which we use only to create and identify your account. Jot requests no name and no email address from Apple, so we never receive either — not even a private relay address.
  • If you sign in with Google, we receive a Google identity token and your Google account ID, which we use only to create and identify your account. We do not request or store your name, email address, or profile photo.

Content you create

What happens to your content depends on which feature you use:

  • Notes/memos ("jots"), including any titles, dates, categories, and list items (e.g. films, books, places you're tracking), are synced to our backend and linked to your account identity so they're available across your signed-in devices.
  • Bills, invoices, and other documents you import are read by our AI provider (see "Third-Party Services" below) to identify structured details (company, amount, dates). What is sent depends on the file: a PDF that carries its own text is sent as that text; a photo, or a scanned PDF of up to four pages, is sent as pictures; a longer scan is read on your device with on-device text recognition, and only that text is sent. Each document is filed with a note of its own, and the details Jot confirms travel inside that note, so they are synced and encrypted like any other note (see below). The full text read from the file stays only on your device. The imported file itself is kept on your device, and on Jot Pro it is also backed up encrypted (see "Files" below).
  • Pictures you share into Jot or attach in the chat (a photo of a receipt, a poster, a thing you want to remember) are sent to our AI provider (see "Third-Party Services" below), which reads the picture to turn it into a memo. A very large picture is made smaller first, and the copy Jot keeps is the same picture that was sent. The picture is kept on your device, and on Jot Pro it is also backed up encrypted (see "Files" below).
  • Voice memos are sent as audio: the recording itself is sent — through our backend, to OpenAI's transcription service (see "Third-Party Services" below). The text that comes back becomes the memo. The recording is kept on your device, and on Jot Pro it is also backed up encrypted (see "Files" below). OpenAI transcribes voice memos whichever AI provider is answering your questions.
  • Files — the audio of a voice memo, a bill document you imported, and a picture you shared — are kept on your device. On the free plan they are not backed up anywhere. On Jot Pro they are also backed up to our backend, encrypted on your device first, on the same terms as your note content below. This is about backup only: a voice memo's audio is sent for transcription on every plan, as described above.
  • Questions you ask Jot ("Ask") and the answers you receive are stored only on your device and are never synced to our backend. When you ask through Siri, Apple's Siri also handles your question and Jot's answer (see "Siri, Shortcuts, Spotlight and widgets" below).

Notes/memos synced to our backend are end-to-end encrypted. Your device encrypts the content before it leaves — the text you wrote and everything Jot derived from it, such as the category, tags, amounts, vendors and titles — using a key created on your device that is never sent to us. Alongside the encrypted content our backend stores only what it needs to file it: which account it belongs to, an identifier for the note, when it was created, and whether it came from the one-time "Let's meet" introduction. We cannot read your notes. Neither can our infrastructure providers, and a legal demand for them would return only encrypted data that we have no way to decrypt.

The key is kept in your device's Keychain. If you have iCloud Keychain switched on, Apple carries it to your other devices; it never passes through Jot. This has a consequence worth understanding: if you lose every device that holds the key, along with the iCloud Keychain copy, we cannot recover your backup — there is no copy of the key on our side to restore it with.

Asking a question is different. The encryption protects your backup, not the AI feature. When you ask Jot something, your question and the note content needed to answer it are decrypted on your device and sent to our AI provider (see "Third-Party Services" below), so that content leaves your device readable.

Notes backed up by earlier app versions, before encryption was added, were stored as plain text. When you update to a version that supports encryption, your device re-uploads them encrypted and the readable copies are replaced.

Files are backed up on the same terms, on Jot Pro only. A voice memo's audio and a bill document you imported are encrypted on your device before they are uploaded, with the same key that protects your notes. The file's name, its type and its size are inside the encrypted part — our backend stores only which account it belongs to, an identifier for the file, which note or bill it belongs to, and when it was created. We cannot open your files. On the free plan nothing is backed up at all: your files stay on your device, and a device that loses them cannot get them back from us. Transcription is separate from backup — a voice memo's audio is sent to be transcribed whatever your plan.

If your subscription ends, the files already backed up stay readable to you for 90 days, so you can restore or export them. After that they are deleted from our backend. Your own copies on your device are never touched by this. Your notes are not affected — they are kept as long as your account is.

Usage and subscription information

  • Number of actions used and your remaining action balance.
  • Subscription status (free or Jot Pro) and renewal state.
  • Basic diagnostic information needed to operate the service (e.g. request timestamps, error logs).
  • How the app's features are used, and reports when the app crashes — described in full under "Analytics and Crash Reporting" below, including how to switch it off.

Jot does not receive or store your payment card details. Purchases are handled entirely by the Apple App Store; subscription status is confirmed to us through our payments partner, RevenueCat (see below).

How Your Information Is Used

  • To store and sync your notes across your signed-in devices.
  • To answer questions you ask about your own notes ("Ask"). This is done by searching your notes on your device, then sending your question (and any relevant note content needed to answer it) to our AI provider (OpenAI, or Anthropic in some configurations) to generate a response. See "Third-Party Services" below.
  • To extract structured details (company, amount, dates) from documents you import: the document's text, or pictures of its pages, is sent to the same AI provider to identify these details. "Content you create" above says which is sent for which kind of file.
  • To turn a picture you share or attach into a memo: the picture is sent to the same AI provider, which reads it and identifies the memo's details.
  • To turn a voice memo into text: the recording is sent through our backend to OpenAI's transcription service, and the text it returns becomes the memo.
  • To operate the actions and subscription system (tracking usage, granting Jot Pro actions, renewing subscriptions).
  • To maintain and improve the reliability of the app (debugging, fraud prevention, abuse prevention).
  • To understand which features are used and to find and fix crashes. See "Analytics and Crash Reporting" below, including how to switch it off.
  • To show ads to users on the free plan. See "Advertising" below.

Your notes are never used for advertising. No note, question, answer, or bill detail is ever sent to an advertising network, used to choose which ad you see, or used to build a profile about you. We do not sell your personal information.

Advertising

If you are on the free plan, Jot shows ads supplied by Google AdMob. They appear as occasional sponsored rows in your list of all memos and in search results, clearly labelled "Sponsored". No ad is ever shown inside a chat.

Jot Pro removes all ads. If you subscribe, the advertising SDK is not started at all, and you are not asked for the consent or tracking permissions described below.

To show ads, Google receives information from your device — including your device's advertising identifier, approximate location derived from your IP address, and technical details about your device and the ad shown. This is collected by Google, not passed on by us: we send Google nothing about you and nothing from your vault.

Your consent, and how to change it

  • If you are in the European Economic Area or the United Kingdom, Jot shows Google's consent form before any ad is requested, and your choice there decides whether ads are personalised. You can change it at any time from the privacy options in the app.
  • On iOS, Jot also asks for permission to track using Apple's App Tracking Transparency prompt. Declining is fine — you will still see ads, but they will not be personalised, and no advertising identifier is shared. You can change this at any time in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking.

Google's handling of this data is governed by its own privacy policy — see How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services.

Analytics and Crash Reporting

Jot records how the app is used, and reports when the app crashes, so that problems can be found and fixed. This is the only part of Jot that exists to tell us something about you rather than to do something for you, so it is worth being exact about what it does and does not include.

What is recorded. A short, fixed list of events, each one a count of something happening: a memo was saved, a question was asked, a voice memo was recorded, a bill was imported, the upgrade screen was shown, a purchase completed, sign-in succeeded, the introduction was finished or skipped. Alongside an event we record only values drawn from a fixed list — which category a memo was filed under, whether it came from typing or from voice, roughly how long a recording was, and which screen raised the upgrade prompt.

What is never recorded. The text of your memos, and their titles, tags, amounts, vendors and dates. The questions you ask and the answers you receive. The names or the contents of your files. None of this is sent to the analytics or crash-reporting services under any circumstances. The list of events is fixed in the app's source code and cannot be extended while the app is running, so no change on our side can start collecting your content without a new release.

Jot does not record your screen. Session replay — the feature some analytics tools offer that reconstructs what a user saw and tapped — is switched off deliberately, because Jot puts your memos on screen almost everywhere.

What identifies you. Events carry the same account identifier that our backend files your notes under. It is not your name and it is not your email address, because we hold neither. Before you sign in it is the random device identifier described under "Account information" above. The analytics service also receives your device model, your operating system version, your app version, and the IP address your device connects from, from which it derives an approximate location (country or city level).

Crash reports contain the technical state of the app at the moment it stopped: which code was running, your device model and operating system version, and the recent events listed above as a trail leading up to the crash. They do not contain your content.

Turning it off

You can switch all of this off at any time in Profile → Data & storage → Share diagnostics. Turning it off stops both the usage events and the crash reports from that device. Nothing else about how Jot works changes.

Where Your Information Is Stored

Your notes/memos are synced to our backend (built on Convex) so they're available if you reinstall the app or sign in on a new device — this is the durable copy of your data, and it is encrypted (see "Content you create" above). A working copy is also kept on your device using Apple's on-device storage (SwiftData), in readable form, so that search and offline access work.

Your "Ask" conversation history (your questions and Jot's answers) is kept only on your device and is not synced to our backend. For a document you import, the details Jot confirms sync inside its note, as described above; the full text read from the file stays on your device.

Siri, Shortcuts, Spotlight and widgets

These features show your notes outside the Jot app itself. Each is run by Apple's system on your device:

  • Siri and Shortcuts. When you save a memo or ask Jot a question through Siri, Siri turns your speech into text and passes it to Jot, then speaks or shows Jot's reply. What you say and the reply are handled by Apple under Apple's own privacy policy, as for any app you use with Siri. Jot also tells the system when you save or open a note in the app, so that Siri can suggest those actions later.
  • Spotlight. The titles and text of your notes are added to the Spotlight search index on your device, so that a search on your iPhone can find them. Apple's system features may use what is in that index, under Apple's own privacy policy.
  • Widgets. To fill the widgets you add, Jot keeps a copy of up to 40 of your notes in a file on your device that only Jot and its widgets can read. On the Lock Screen and in StandBy, note text stays hidden until you choose to show it with the widget's lock icon.

Deleting a note, or signing out, removes it from Spotlight and from the widgets. You can stop Siri and Search from using Jot at any time in your device's Settings, in Jot's Siri options.

International Transfers

We are based in Poland, but some of the providers above (OpenAI, Anthropic, Convex, RevenueCat, Google and Apple) are established in the United States, so your data may be processed outside the European Economic Area. Analytics data is an exception: Jot uses PostHog's European Union region, so it stays in the EU. Crash reports go to Google Firebase Crashlytics and may be processed in the United States. Where that happens, the transfer is covered by an appropriate safeguard under Chapter V of the GDPR — normally the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses included in our agreements with those providers, or the EU–US Data Privacy Framework where the provider is certified. You can request more detail about the safeguards in place by emailing us.

California Residents

We do not sell your personal information for money, and we never share the contents of your notes with anyone for advertising.

However, if you are on the free plan and you allow tracking when iOS asks, the advertising identifier made available to Google AdMob may be used for personalised advertising. Under the CCPA/CPRA this counts as "sharing" for cross-context behavioural advertising. To opt out, decline the App Tracking Transparency prompt, or turn Jot off under iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking at any time. Subscribing to Jot Pro also stops it entirely, because no advertising SDK runs. We do not knowingly share the personal information of anyone under 16 for these purposes.

California residents may request access to or deletion of their personal information using the same email address above, and will not be discriminated against for exercising those rights.

Data Retention

We retain your account and note content for as long as your account is active, so that your notes remain available to you. Files backed up on Jot Pro are kept while the subscription is active, and for 90 days after it ends, after which they are deleted. If you request account deletion, we will delete your stored content and account identifiers, except where we are required to retain limited records (e.g. for fraud prevention or legal compliance). Accounting records relating to a purchase are kept for the period required by Polish tax law (currently five years from the end of the tax year). Diagnostic and error logs are kept only as long as needed to investigate problems, and are routinely discarded. Usage events are retained by PostHog for as long as the project is active; crash reports are retained by Firebase Crashlytics for 90 days. If you switch diagnostics off, no further events or reports are sent, and you can ask us to delete what was already collected using the email address below.

We do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone below the age that applies to them. If we learn that we have, we will delete the account and its data. If you are a parent or guardian and believe your child has provided us with personal information, contact us at veret.evg@gmail.com and we will delete it.
